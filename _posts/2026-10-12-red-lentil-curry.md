## Ingredients (2 people)

- 200 g red lentils (rode linzen), dry weight
- 2 to 3 tbsp Indian curry paste, such as tikka masala or madras (currypasta)
- 1 tin of coconut milk, 400 ml (kokosmelk)
- 1 tin of chopped tomatoes, 400 g (tomatenblokjes)
- 1 sweet pepper
- 150 g spinach (spinazie)
- 1 onion
- 2 cloves of garlic
- 1 red chilli (rode peper)
- 150 g rice, dry weight, or 2 naan breads

<!-- -->

- 2 tbsp oil
- 300 ml vegetable stock
- salt

## Recipe

Total 40 min<br>
Vegan with rice, vegetarian
{: .recipe-facts }

Rinse the lentils in a sieve until the water runs clear. Finely chop the onion,
garlic and chilli, taking the seeds out of the chilli if you want less heat, and
dice the pepper.

Warm the oil in a large pan and soften the onion over a medium heat for five
minutes, then add the garlic, chilli and pepper for another two. Stir in the
curry paste and let it fry for a minute until it smells fragrant.

Add the lentils, tomatoes, coconut milk and stock, bring it to the boil and turn
it down to a gentle simmer. Cook it uncovered for about twenty minutes, stirring
often so the lentils do not catch on the bottom, until they have fallen apart
into a thick sauce; add a splash of water if it gets too stiff. Put the rice on
while it simmers.

Stir in the spinach at the end and let it wilt for a minute, then taste for
salt. Serve over the rice, or with warm naan for scooping.

## Notes

Check the curry paste: Indian pastes are normally vegetarian, but Thai red curry
paste usually contains shrimp paste (garnalenpasta), so read the label. Naan is
often made with yogurt or ghee, which is fine here but not vegan. The pepper,
spinach and tinned tomatoes fill in the vegetables; carrot, courgette, cauliflower
or sweet potato all work too. The quantities are inferred and approximate.
